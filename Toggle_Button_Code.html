How this is calculated +

1 · Cells per frame

The frame's comb area (both sides) is multiplied by an empirical constant K, which folds in how much of the frame the comb actually covers and how irregular real cells are compared to a perfect hexagon:

N_frame = 2 × K × L × W  (K = 3.683 × 10⁻²)

K was derived from photographing and measuring 10 real hive frames (300 sampled cells), combining an area-fraction constant (C ≈ 0.874) and an irregularity-correction factor (I ≈ 0.642), divided by the average cell area (≈15.24 mm²).

Total cells = N_frame × Number of frames

2 · Honey volume by comb age

Not every cell stores honey — some hold brood or pollen — and the volume each storage cell can hold shrinks as comb ages. The paper computes this per hive by multiplying estimated cells by the ratio of storage cells to total cells, then by the volume a cell of that age can hold, and summing across frames:

V_hive = Σ (N_frame × N_storage⁄N_actual) × V_age

This calculator uses the average mL-per-cell rate the study's own 10-frame trial produced at each comb age (aggregated from its honey-yield tables), applied to your estimated cell count:

  • 3 years: ≈ 0.0416 mL/cell
  • 4 years: ≈ 0.0395 mL/cell
  • 5 years: ≈ 0.0382 mL/cell
  • 6 years: ≈ 0.0341 mL/cell
